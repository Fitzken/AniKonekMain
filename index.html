01The main problem · Ang problema

Despite being the foundation of our food supply, farmers capture the smallest share.

Magsasaka ang pinakamaliit ang bahagi sa retail na presyo.

2025 was a big harvest year. But the price farmers received fell to a 3-year low, while store prices kept climbing. Despite growing the food we all depend on, farm families often receive the smallest portion of what consumers pay — with many remaining among the poorest households in the country.

₱17.70per kg — what farmers got for palay in 2025. The lowest in 3 years. · PSA
₱49.30per kg — what we paid for regular milled rice in Jul 2026. · PSA
↓ 22.7%palay price drop in Oct 2025 vs the year before — even with a record harvest. · PSA

Big harvest. Small income. That is the problem.

Why does this happen?

  • Few buyers near the farm. When only one trader buys in a village, farmers must accept the price offered — there is no second offer to compare.
  • Long chains. Palay rides a truck to a mill, then a warehouse, then a store. Every stop adds cost, and most of it is deducted from the farmer's price.
  • Food loss. About 14% of food spoils before it reaches the store (FAO). Spoiled food is work the farmer is never paid for.
  • Cheap imports. When imported rice floods the market, local palay prices fall — even in a record harvest year.

The good news: we can help — and benefit too. See below.

From farm to your table — many stops, more cost.

05The win-win · Sabay panalo

Buying direct helps both of you.

A shorter road means fresher food for you and a bigger share for the farmer. Sometimes it also means lower prices — especially when harvests are abundant.

For you

  • Fresher food. It was harvested nearby and spent less time on the road.
  • Possibly cheaper. Fewer stops means fewer markups between farm and table.
  • You know your farmer. You can ask what was grown, how, and when.

For the farmer

  • Bigger share. More of every peso you spend goes to the grower.
  • Fairer prices. More buyers means farmers compare offers instead of accepting just one.
  • Next season's capital. Steady income buys seeds, fertilizer and school fees — so the farm continues.

KADIWA ng Pangulo — direct markets

The Department of Agriculture's KADIWA program sets up pop-up markets where farmer and fisherfolk groups sell straight to consumers — no long chain in between. It gives producers an extra selling option and gives you affordable local food. It already works at scale:

10What you can do · Gawin

Five simple moves.

You don't need to be perfect — you need to be deliberate. Start with one move this week.

Buy direct

From farmers, co-ops, farmers' markets and KADIWA. Even one purchase a week sends demand back to the farm.

Buy local

Choose Philippine-grown food. Shorter roads mean fresher food and more money staying in your community.

Buy in season

Use the calendar above. In-season crops travel less and taste better.

Waste less

Store food well and cook what you buy. Waste undone is harvest honored.

Back co-ops

Cooperatives give farmers storage, capital and a stronger voice in pricing.
